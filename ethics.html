ETH-01 Medium 150 pts Unsolved

In Scope, Out of Scope

You are given an engagement letter for a penetration test and a list of six actions a tester took. Decide which ones the letter actually permits.

Show hint

Read the scope clause literally. A subdomain that resolves to a third-party host, and any test outside the agreed window, are not covered however convenient they are.

What this challenge taught you

Authorization is specific, written, and bounded by systems, methods, and time. Acting outside it removes your legal protection entirely, no matter how good your intentions were.

ETH-02 Medium 150 pts Unsolved

The Disclosure Dilemma

While solving an unrelated challenge you find a genuine flaw in a live university service that exposes student records. Choose the responsible sequence of actions.

Show hint

Ask at each step whether the action reduces harm to the people whose data is exposed, and whether it gives the owner a fair chance to fix the problem.

What this challenge taught you

Responsible disclosure means stopping at proof, not extraction; reporting privately to the owner; giving reasonable time to remediate; and never using the finding for advantage. Confirming a flaw by downloading real records causes the harm you were trying to prevent.

What You Take Away

Ethics is the part of this course that stays relevant no matter which tools you end up using.

  • Get it in writing

    Verbal permission is not authorization. Scope, methods, and dates belong in a signed document.

  • Stop at proof

    Demonstrate a vulnerability with the minimum access needed. Do not extract data to prove a point.

  • Minimise harm

    Prefer the action that protects the people whose data is at risk, even when a louder option is available.

  • Accept accountability

    Professional codes of conduct bind you to honesty about what you did, what you found, and what you could not test.
