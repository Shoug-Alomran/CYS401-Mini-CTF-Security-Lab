Workshop 04 · Cybersecurity Ethics

Know Where the Line Is

Authorization, scope, consent, and responsible disclosure: what separates a security professional from an attacker using the same tools.

What You’ll Practice

Skill without judgement is a liability. This track covers the professional obligations that come with the rest of the lab.

01

Authorization & Scope

Written permission defines which systems, which methods, and which window. Outside it, testing is an offence.

02

Responsible Disclosure

Reporting a flaw to the owner, allowing time to fix, and publishing only what helps defenders.

03

Law & Professional Duty

Computer misuse and data protection law, plus the codes of conduct that bind practitioners.

What You Take Away

Ethics is the part of this course that stays relevant no matter which tools you end up using.

  • Get it in writing

    Verbal permission is not authorization. Scope, methods, and dates belong in a signed document.

  • Stop at proof

    Demonstrate a vulnerability with the minimum access needed. Do not extract data to prove a point.

  • Minimise harm

    Prefer the action that protects the people whose data is at risk, even when a louder option is available.

  • Accept accountability

    Professional codes of conduct bind you to honesty about what you did, what you found, and what you could not test.
